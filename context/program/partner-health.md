# Partner Health & Relationship Quality

## What Makes a Great Partner
A partner Bryce loves working with:
- Has a good attitude and is **joyful**
- Asks engaging, curious questions
- Approaches the partnership as a **collaboration** — solving problems together
- Builds genuine rapport; finds things to connect on beyond just the logistics

### Team-Validated Best Practices (from internal Slack thread)
These are the markers of a thriving parish partnership, ranked roughly by impact:

1. **Pastor enthusiasm and buy-in** — the single biggest lever. When the pastor is excited from the front, it moves the whole parish. (Mentioned first *and* last by Jonathan Campbell.)
2. **A dedicated implementation team** — a small "Mission Team" of staff or volunteers who take on Hallow as their main project. WOO (Winning Others Over) personalities are ideal.
3. **Tech savvy, or willingness to learn** — doesn't need to be advanced; basic comfort with apps and web technology is enough.
4. **Constant, multi-channel promotion** — bulletins, flyers, pew cards, stands, TV screens, word of mouth, Mass announcements. Think "Hallow Hub." Not a one-time push.
5. **Intentional prayer challenge launches** — promoted in advance, tied to Mass messages, adoration, personal invite goals. Not just turned on and forgotten.
6. **Parish staff actively engaging on the parish page** — prayer requests, reflections, small groups. Pastor engagement on the page is especially powerful.
7. **Prayer request activity** — one of the strongest engagement signals. Takes time to build, but parish leaders modeling it accelerates it.
8. **Admin Dashboard monitoring** — checking weekly. Pastors are often amazed when they see actual usage data; it builds momentum.
9. **A successful in-person event** — a Growth Event or Mission can be a turning point if the parish is primed for it.

> Real example (Lizzie Bagley): A parish hit 30%+ activation just 3 weeks in — driven by a young volunteer doing announcements after every Mass (including weekdays), a 6-person Mission Team, and a pastor with realistic long-term expectations.

## What Makes a Draining Partner
A partner that creates friction:
- Makes **constant complaints** about the partnership
- Struggles significantly with **technology**, requiring heavy hand-holding
- Asks for things Hallow **cannot provide** — outside scope, outside tier, or outside mission
