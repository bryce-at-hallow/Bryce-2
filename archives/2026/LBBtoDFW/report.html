The Core Issue

Lubbock Is a Bottleneck.

Lubbock Preston Smith International Airport (LBB) has limited direct routes. Nearly every flight to an East Coast, Midwest, or non-Texas destination requires a connecting stop — almost always through Dallas/Fort Worth. This creates a compounding tax on every trip I take for Hallow.

⏱
Added Time on Every Trip
Each connecting flight through DFW adds 2–4 hours per trip — time not spent with parish partners, preparing to lead events, or recovering between back-to-back weeks on the road.
  • → LBB→DFW flight + layover: ~1.5–2.5 hrs added each way
  • → 6 of 9 trips in 2026 required a DFW connection
  • → Est. 20+ hours lost to connecting travel YTD
  • → Limited flight windows = less flexible scheduling
💸
An Avoidable Company Cost
The LBB→DFW connecting segment is an additional expense on every flight-based trip — a cost that wouldn't exist if I were based at a major hub like DFW.
  • → Avg connecting round trip: $175–$300
  • → 6 connecting trips in 2026 YTD: ~$1,050–$1,800
  • → Annualized (30 flight trips/yr): ~$5,000–$9,000
  • → All of it disappears with a DFW address
The Solution

Why DFW Changes the Math.

Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport is one of the most connected airports in the world. For a role built around reaching parishes across the entire United States, it is the ideal base of operations — and arguably the most strategic home city I could be in for this job.

  • ✓ Direct flights to virtually every major U.S. city — no more connecting hops
  • ✓ American Airlines' primary hub — Hallow's carrier of record
  • ✓ Greater scheduling flexibility and more booking options
  • ✓ Central time zone — optimal for team communication coast to coast
  • ✓ Better rested and more prepared when I arrive at parish events
  • ✓ Saves the company an estimated $5,000–$9,000/year in connecting flight costs
  • ✓ Access to the DFW Catholic market — one of the largest in the country
  • ✓ Easier to host or attend in-person team meetups
